# Partner Acquisition

> Partner acquisition, not customer acquisition, is the hard part of Phase 1. Customers arrive through partners. If we sign 10 dietitians, the customers follow. If we sign 2, no amount of marketing rescues the pilot.
>
> Sequence matters: **dietitians → suppliers → couriers → customers.** Never recruit customers before there is capacity to serve them.

---

# 1. Dietitian Acquisition

## Goal

**Find and sign the first 10 dietitians in Atakum.** Target: 5+ actively generating orders by the end of the pilot.

Signed ≠ active. Expect roughly half of signed partners to never generate a second order. Plan for that: recruit 10 to get 5.

## Channels

### Google Maps — the systematic base
Search "diyetisyen Atakum", "beslenme danışmanı Samsun", "diyet kliniği Atakum". Build a spreadsheet of every result: name, clinic, address, phone, Instagram, review count, review sentiment.

This gives a finite, complete list — likely 40–60 practitioners across Atakum and adjacent İlkadım. It is not a big number. Every one can be contacted personally.

**Prioritise:** independent practitioners over hospital-employed (they control their own client relationships and decisions); active reviewers (they care about reputation); those visibly running transformation programs.

### Instagram — qualification and warm entry
Most independent dietitians in Turkey run their practice through Instagram. Their feed tells you what you need to know before you contact them:

- Do they post client before/after results? → they are outcome-focused; strong fit
- Do they post meal plans and grocery guidance? → they already feel the adherence problem
- Do they sell online programs? → they understand digital tooling
- Follower count 1k–20k → serious practice, not yet an influencer with different incentives

**Approach:** engage genuinely for a few days before messaging. A cold DM converts poorly; a DM from someone who has been reading their content converts.

**First message — short, specific, no pitch:**
> "Merhaba [name], Atakum'da diyetisyenlerle çalışan bir proje kuruyorum — danışanların planlarını daha tutarlı uygulamalarını sağlayan bir sistem. Fikrinizi almak isterim, satış amaçlı değil. 20 dakikalık bir görüşme için müsait olur musunuz?"
>
> *("I'm building a project working with dietitians in Atakum — a system that helps clients follow their plans more consistently. I'd like your opinion, not to sell you anything. Would you have 20 minutes?")*

Asking for an opinion converts far better than asking for a partnership, and produces better information. In weeks 1–2 we genuinely are asking for opinions (see [`VALIDATION_PLAN.md`](./VALIDATION_PLAN.md)).

### Local healthcare networks — the compounding channel
- Ondokuz Mayıs University nutrition and dietetics department: academics, graduates, and the local professional community all pass through it
- Local dietitian associations and professional WhatsApp/Telegram groups
- Private clinics and polyclinics with in-house dietitians
- Personal trainers, who know every dietitian in town and will happily introduce you

**The critical dynamic:** dietitians in a mid-size city all know each other. The first 2–3 signings determine whether the rest of the market hears "this is legitimate" or nothing at all. **Over-invest in the first three partners.** Make them successful, visibly. They are the reference.

## Value Proposition

### What we say

> **"We help your clients follow your plans more consistently."**

### What we do not say

> ~~"Sell products through us and earn commission."~~

This framing is not a marketing preference — it is a filter. A dietitian who signs up for commission is the wrong partner. They will inflate baskets, push products, and treat the platform as a revenue line. The dietitian we want signs up because their clients keep quitting and they know exactly why.

**Commission is secondary.** Mention it late in the conversation, and frame it as compensation for the plan-review work they do, not as an earnings opportunity. If a prospect's first question is about commission rates, that is a signal to qualify carefully.

### The primary value, in the order they care about it

**1. Better customer retention — their actual business problem.**

A solo dietitian's economics:
- Client pays a monthly consultation fee
- Average client life: 4–8 weeks
- Most working hours go to acquiring replacements, not practising

Doubling average client life from 6 to 12 weeks roughly doubles practice revenue with zero additional acquisition effort. That is the pitch, and it should be delivered as arithmetic, not adjectives.

**2. Better results — their professional identity.**

Dietitians are judged on outcomes they do not control. They write a correct plan; the client shops badly, portions wrong, substitutes freely, and the plan fails. The dietitian gets the blame and, worse, starts doubting their own work. Removing the execution gap means their plans finally get tested as written.

**3. Professional service improvement — their competitive position.**

- Adherence visibility: they can see who approved, who skipped, who is drifting — before the client disappears
- Time recovered: no more answering "which brand", "how many grams", "can I swap this"
- Differentiation: "my clients get their food supplied to their door" is a strong claim against every other dietitian in Atakum
- A tool that makes them look modern to their own clients

**4. Commission (~5%).** Mentioned last. Real, meaningful at scale, but never the headline.

## The Conversation

**Do:**
- Meet in person at their clinic. This is a relationship business in a mid-size city.
- Open with their problem, not our product: *"How many of your clients are still with you after two months?"* Then be quiet.
- Show the retention dashboard mockup. It is the single most persuasive artefact we have.
- Be explicit about boundaries: their clients remain their clients; we never sell them another dietitian; plan content is never used commercially.
- Offer to start with 3–5 clients, not their whole book. Low commitment, fast evidence.
- Ask what would make the tool unusable for them, and actually change it.

**Do not:**
- Lead with commission
- Promise results before the pilot has produced any
- Ask for exclusivity — we have not earned it
- Charge them anything. The dashboard is free, permanently, for partners who generate orders.
- Oversell the software. It is deliberately minimal in Phase 1, and pretending otherwise creates disappointment at first login.

## Onboarding

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Agreement | One page. Commission terms, data boundaries, no exclusivity, either side can exit. |
| 2. Account setup | We create it and configure it with them, in person. |
| 3. Plan templates | Sit with them and build 2–3 of *their own* plan templates. This is the highest-value hour in the whole onboarding — it makes the tool immediately faster than their current method. |
| 4. First clients | They pick 3–5 clients who are struggling with adherence. We help write the invitation message. |
| 5. First order | Walk through it together, live. |
| 6. Week 2 check-in | In person or by phone. The second week is where partners silently drop off. |
| 7. Week 4 review | Show them their own retention numbers. This is the moment they either become believers or churn. |

## Tracking

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Dietitians contacted | 40+ |
| Meetings held | 20+ |
| Signed | 10 |
| Generated ≥1 order | 8 |
| Active in final pilot month | 5+ |
| Customers per active dietitian | 8–15 |
| Client activation rate (invited → paying) | ≥30% |

# 2. Fitness Centre Acquisition

## Goal

2–3 partner centres in Atakum. This is a **channel**, not a revenue line — the objective is qualified customer flow and local credibility.

## Targets

Gyms, CrossFit boxes, personal training studios, pilates and functional studios in Atakum. Prioritise those already selling transformation or body-composition packages — they have already sold the outcome and cannot deliver the nutrition half of it.

**Reach the owner or the PT team lead.** Front desk staff cannot make this decision and will not pass it upward.

## Value Proposition

> **"Transform your membership into a complete lifestyle program."**

**Their problem:** gyms sell body transformation and control roughly 30% of the inputs. Nutrition drives the rest and they have no influence over it. Members do not see results, blame the gym, and cancel. Member churn is the defining economic problem of the gym business.

**What we offer:**

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Complete product** | Training + dietitian + food supply, sold as one package |
| **Higher-tier pricing** | A premium tier that is genuinely differentiated, not just more sessions |
| **Member results** | Members who actually change look like the gym's achievement — because partly it is |
| **Retention** | Their core metric, improved by the input they could never control |
| **Zero operational burden** | We handle everything; they refer |
| **Revenue share** | Per referred active customer, monthly. Simple to administer, aligned with retention. |
| **On-site dietitian** | Optionally host a partner dietitian one day a week. Costs the gym nothing, closes deals in person, benefits all three parties. |

## Structures (choose per partner)

1. **Referral** — gym refers members, receives a monthly fee per active customer. Simplest; start here.
2. **Bundled package** — gym sells "Transformation Package" including nutrition supply at a wholesale rate, keeps the spread. Higher value, needs more trust.
3. **On-site dietitian day** — partner dietitian holds consultations at the gym. Strongest conversion, requires a good dietitian partner first.

## Approach

- Visit in person, off-peak
- Ask about member retention before mentioning anything we do
- Propose a trial: 10 members, no commitment, 8 weeks
- Provide everything: posters, a QR sign-up flow, a short PT briefing, member-facing copy
- Report results back to the owner monthly, in numbers

# 3. Courier Acquisition

## The Strategic Position

**We do not compete with Getir-style instant delivery.**

That fight is unwinnable and unnecessary. Instant-delivery platforms compete on availability and per-drop volume with deep capital behind them. We are not bidding for the same work on the same terms — we are offering a different kind of work to the same labour pool.

## Our Advantage: Planned Delivery

**The courier knows, before the shift begins:**
- Exactly which deliveries they have
- Exactly where they go
- The full route, sequenced
- The time windows
- The total duration
- **The total pay**

Nothing in instant delivery offers that.

| | Instant delivery | Planned route |
|---|---|---|
| Work visibility | None — wait for dispatch | Whole route known in advance |
| Idle time | High, unpaid | Effectively none |
| Income | Per drop, variable, weather-dependent | Guaranteed shift floor + per-stop |
| Route quality | Random dispatch, backtracking | Clustered, sequenced, dense |
| Schedule | Always-on availability expected | Fixed days, chosen shifts |
| Stress | Constant timer pressure | One list, one route |
| Fuel efficiency | Poor — scattered destinations | Good — tight clusters |

### Benefits, stated as we state them to couriers

**Less waiting.** No sitting on the bike waiting for an order. Arrive, load, ride, finish.

**Predictable income.** You know before you start what the shift pays. The guaranteed shift floor means a slow day is not a lost day.

**Better route efficiency.** Deliveries are clustered in Atakum, not scattered across Samsun. More drops per hour, less fuel per drop.

**Planned schedule.** Fixed delivery days. You can hold another job, study, or plan your week — instant delivery makes that impossible.

## Potential Sources

**Independent motorcycle couriers.** Already own the vehicle, know the city, work for several clients. Our shifts fit alongside their existing work rather than replacing it.

**Existing delivery workers wanting additional income.** People working for restaurants or instant-delivery platforms who want predictable supplementary hours on their off days. Often the best hires — trained, equipped, and immediately able to compare our terms against the alternative.

**Local courier communities.** WhatsApp and Telegram groups, motorcycle clubs, courier gathering points. Word of mouth moves fast here. One satisfied courier recruits the next two, and if we treat couriers badly the same channel will tell everyone.

**Also viable:** university students with vehicles (limited hours, good with the app, reliable for fixed weekly shifts), and small local delivery firms for van-based consolidated routes as volume grows.

## Requirements

- Own vehicle (motorcycle or small car), valid licence and insurance
- Smartphone
- Available on the fixed delivery days
- Presentable and courteous — for a subscription customer the courier is the only human touchpoint, every week
- Willing to handle chilled goods correctly

## Terms

- Independent contractor
- **700 TL shift guarantee + 50 TL per delivery** (*planning assumption — validate in week 1–2 interviews*)
- Route and expected pay visible before the shift starts
- Weekly payment
- Insulated delivery bags provided
- Fuel is the courier's cost, factored into the rate

## Approach

1. Interview 6–8 couriers in weeks 1–2. Do not pitch — ask what they earn, what they hate, and what would make a route worth taking. The terms above are a hypothesis to be corrected by these conversations.
2. Onboard 3. Two run routes, one is redundancy.
3. Run the first routes alongside them personally. Founders should deliver in week 5. You learn more about the operation in one route than in a month of dashboards.
4. Pay on time, every time, without exception. In this labour market, reliable payment is the strongest retention tool that exists.

## Tracking

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Couriers interviewed | 6–8 |
| Onboarded | 3 |
| Retained at week 8 | ≥2 |
| Deliveries per route | ≥10 |
| On-time rate | ≥90% |
| Failed delivery rate | ≤3% |
| Courier-caused complaints | <2% of deliveries |

# 4. Supplier Acquisition (Brief)

Covered operationally in [`OPERATIONS.md`](./OPERATIONS.md); the acquisition side in short:

**Target 2–4 suppliers total.** Butcher, chicken supplier, greengrocer/market, and one dairy/dry goods source. Fewer suppliers means tighter quality control, which is the highest-severity Phase 1 risk.

**The pitch:** predictable weekly volume, visible days in advance, no customer acquisition cost, batch preparation instead of walk-in trade, and one consolidated payer with agreed terms.

**Select on:** consistency over price, willingness to portion to spec, hygiene, a reachable named contact, and capacity headroom for 3–5× growth.

**Agree in writing:** portion tolerances, quality spec, pickup-ready times, pricing for the term, and what happens when they cannot fulfil.

# 5. Acquisition Sequence

```
Week 1-2   Dietitian interviews (20+)  ──┐
           Supplier discovery           ─┼─> understand the market,
           Courier interviews           ──┘   correct the assumptions

Week 3-4   Sign 5-10 dietitians  ──> they invite first clients
           Sign 2-4 suppliers
           Onboard 3 couriers
           Sign 1-2 fitness centres

Week 5-8   Customers arrive through dietitians
           Continue dietitian recruitment (the funnel never stops)
           Weekly partner check-ins
```

**Never recruit customers ahead of capacity.** A customer served badly in week 1 is a churned customer and a damaged dietitian relationship — and in a district the size of Atakum, that story travels.
